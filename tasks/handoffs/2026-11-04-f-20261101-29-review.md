### plan-r1.md

# Plan: Chess.com account sync of a very large history completes in bounded chunks

## Goal

A Chess.com account sync whose full history exceeds one export's byte, archive-count or time
bound imports that history instead of failing: each export publishes a complete prefix of whole
months, the existing coverage cursor records it, and the same account job continues from that
cursor until the history is covered. A failure after some chunks keeps the published chunks and
the next sync resumes from the cursor instead of starting over.

## MANDATE

Finding f-20261101-29 (verbatim):

* **Where:** `src-tauri/src/chesscom.rs:44,411-416` (100 MiB `MAX_PGN_BYTES` over the whole export), `chesscom.rs:40,43` (15-minute export deadline, 16 MiB per month), `db/linked_collections/batch.rs:509-531` (cursor saved only after a successful publish).
* **Defect:** whole-run limits fail staging before anything is published, and without a cursor each retry refetches the full history.
* **Failure scenario:** an account with roughly 40,000+ games (about 1.5-2.5 KB each with clocks) gets "Chess.com PGN export is too large" on every Download and never imports any history.
* **Open question:** publish per month (or up to the last complete month under the cap) and let the cursor carry the rest; how does that interact with the pending-batch recovery contract?

## Threat model and non-goals

Input adversary: none beyond today's. Chess.com responses stay untrusted and keep every existing
bound (URL validation, 512 KiB index, 16 MiB per archive response, 30 s per request, rule-set
filter). Environments: linked collections (Linux/macOS) and the unlinked account database
(all platforms, the only account path on Windows). The real load is an honest account with
tens of thousands of games, or 20 years of monthly archives, on a slow or rate-limited link.
Non-goals: Lichess export limits; changing what a sync imports, how games are deduplicated, or
how the cursor is computed; any renderer or UI change.

## Traced premises

* `chesscom.rs:360-454` — one `tokio::time::timeout(EXPORT_TIMEOUT = 15 min)` wraps index fetch,
  every archive fetch and staging; `index.archives.len() > MAX_ARCHIVES (240)` fails before any
  month is fetched (`:371`); `total_pgn_bytes > MAX_PGN_BYTES (100 MiB)` over all months fails
  (`:422-427`). Any of these returns an error before `install_staged_pgn_artifact` (`:459`), so
  nothing is published and no receipt is recorded.
* Archives are sorted ascending by `(year, month)` and filtered to `>= first_archive_month(since_ms)`
  (`:379-383`); `first_archive_month` is the UTC month of the cursor (`:179-191`), i.e. the cursor
  month is re-fetched inclusively. One archive response is at most 16 MiB of JSON
  (`MAX_ARCHIVE_BYTES`, `:43`), so one month's staged PGN is always far below 100 MiB.
* The cursor is the newest game *start* time in the export, never below `since`:
  `account_sync_cursor` (`db/linked_collections.rs:3524-3542`, non-Lichess returns
  `max(newest, since)`). Linked path: `record_download` computes `through_ms` from the published
  file (`linked_collections.rs:839-958`); `finish_pending` copies it to
  `record.coverage_cursor_ms` after a successful append (`batch.rs:543-563`); the descriptor
  exposes it as `fetch_since_ms` (`linked_collections.rs:2151-2160`), which the renderer sends as
  `since_ms`. Unlinked path: the import stores `AccountSyncCursor` in its transaction
  (`db/mod.rs` `update_info_count(db, "AccountSyncCursor", …)`), and
  `start_account_completion` reads it natively as `since_ms` before the download
  (`infra/operations/accounts.rs:405-423`, `db/mod.rs:1273`).
* Both paths deduplicate by provider game id (Chess.com `Link`): linked merge `append_absent`
  (`batch.rs:50-217`), unlinked `AccountProviderGames` (d-20261103-07). Re-fetching the cursor
  month and any months after it is therefore harmless, which f-20261101-23 already notes for the
  linked Chess.com path ("archives by end month, dedup by provider id").
* Account job: `start_account_completion` (`accounts.rs:382-613`) runs one download, then either
  `append_collection_batch` (linked) or `convert_pgn_blocking` + `delete_empty_games_blocking`
  (unlinked), all under one `OperationLease` cancellation token. Each download takes a fresh
  ticket from `prepare_download`, stores it in `snapshot.progress_id` and emits
  `CompletionChanged` (`:436-448`); the renderer's progress bar follows
  `completion.progressId` (`src/components/home/AccountCard.tsx:179-185`).
* Pending-batch recovery: a batch is staged under an owned leaf and recorded as `pending`
  (`batch.rs:272-373`); `append_batch` publishes it, then `finish_pending` marks it completed,

## Approach

### O1 — A resumable export publishes a complete prefix of whole months

Answers the MANDATE's "publish per month (or up to the last complete month under the cap) and
let the cursor carry the rest".

* The account path asks the Chess.com core for a *resumable* export. It stages months in
  ascending order and adds a month only whole: a month whose staged PGN would push the export
  past `MAX_PGN_BYTES` is not staged in this export, and staging stops there.
* `MAX_ARCHIVES` becomes a per-export bound on the number of months staged, not a refusal of an
  index that lists more months: staging stops after that many months. (Derived from "whole-run
  limits fail staging"; a player active every month since Chess.com's 2007 archives reaches 240
  months during 2027.) The index stays bounded by `MAX_INDEX_BYTES`.
* `EXPORT_TIMEOUT` bounds one export. When it elapses after at least one whole month is staged,
  the in-flight fetch is dropped and the staged months are published; when no month is staged
  yet, the export fails with today's `"Chess.com export deadline exceeded"` and publishes
  nothing.
* A resumable export that stopped for any of these three reasons publishes the staged prefix
  through the unchanged `install_staged_pgn_artifact` and receipt path and reports
  `more_available = true` on `RuntimeDownload`. Every other download (Lichess, a complete
  Chess.com export) reports `false`.
* A resumable export never publishes a partial month and never publishes an empty export when
  months were requested; per-game skipping (rule sets) and the existing empty-archive and
  no-archive errors are unchanged.
* The standalone `download_chess_com_games` command keeps whole-export semantics: over a limit
  it fails exactly as today and publishes nothing. The scope (complete vs resumable) is an
  explicit argument of the runtime, chosen by its two callers, not inferred from
  `fail_progress_on_receipt_error`.

Correctness of the hand-off, from the traced premises: the published prefix ends at month M and
contains every game of months `first_month..=M`; its cursor is the newest start among them,
whose UTC month is `<= M`; the next export starts at that month inclusive, so no month after M
is skipped, and the months re-fetched up to M deduplicate by provider id.

### O2 — The account job continues from the cursor until the history is covered

* After a chunk whose download reported `more_available`, and only after that chunk has fully
  completed its existing lifecycle — linked: `append_collection_batch` returned with
  `index_ready && durability_confirmed` (so `finish_pending` advanced the cursor and cleared the
  pending batch); unlinked: conversion committed and `delete_empty_games_blocking` finished — the
  same account job starts the next chunk with a fresh download, still under the same lease and
  cancellation token.
* The next chunk's `since_ms` is read natively after the previous chunk completed: linked from the
  collection record's current coverage cursor, through the same filter the descriptor uses for
  `fetch_since_ms` (one shared function, both callers routed through it); unlinked from
  `account_sync_cursor_blocking`, as the first chunk already does. The renderer-supplied
  `since_ms` is used only for the first linked chunk, as today.
* Each chunk resets the per-chunk snapshot state it owns (`publication`, `coverage_holds`,
  `committed_database`, `deletion_pending`, `importing`) and clears `request.pending_batch`, then
  takes a new download ticket, updates `progress_id` and emits `CompletionChanged` exactly as the
  first download does.
* The loop stops with success when a chunk reports `more_available = false`. It stops with the
  existing notice result (`collection_notice: true`) when a linked append is committed but not
  `index_ready` or not `durability_confirmed`. Any chunk error ends the job with that error, as
  today.
* Progress guard: the next chunk starts only if the native cursor after the chunk is strictly
  greater than the cursor the chunk started from (absent counts as lowest). Otherwise the job
  fails with a typed error naming that the Chess.com history could not advance, so a chunk can
  never repeat forever. The number of chunks is therefore bounded by the number of archive
  months.
* A retried or recovered attempt (`pending_batch` supplied, or a `deletion_pending` retry) runs
  one chunk only, as today; its continuation comes from the next sync's cursor.
* The download seam in `import_account_games_with_download` / `start_account_completion`
  becomes callable once per chunk; production `download_account` is unchanged apart from the
  scope argument.

### O3 — Pending-batch recovery contract stays per chunk

Answers the MANDATE's "how does that interact with the pending-batch recovery contract?": every
chunk is one ordinary batch with its own receipt, pending record, publish, `finish_pending` and
cursor advance. No chunk starts while a pending batch exists, because O2 starts the next chunk
only after `finish_pending` succeeded. A failure inside chunk k leaves chunks `< k` published
with the cursor advanced, and chunk k in exactly the recovery state a single export leaves
today; the existing Retry finishes it and the next sync continues from the cursor. No new
recovery state, registry field or renderer contract is introduced.

## Goal

A Chess.com account sync whose full history exceeds one export's byte, archive-count or time

months, the existing coverage cursor records it, and the same account job continues from that

### O1a — Coverage is recorded by archive month, not only by game start

A game-start cursor cannot say "months up to M are covered": a slow link or long daily games can
leave the newest start inside the cursor month, so the next export would re-fetch the same
prefix forever (r1 P1).

* A stopped export whose last whole month is M reports a coverage floor equal to 00:00 UTC on the
  first day of month M+1. All archives up to M were fetched whole and every game they hold
  started before that instant, so the floor is a true lower bound of the coverage the export
  establishes.
* The shared `account_sync_cursor` takes the floor as an input and returns at least the floor;
  with no floor its result is unchanged for every provider. Both cursor writers pass it: the
  linked receipt (`record_download` via `record_download_receipt`, from the Chess.com core) and
  the unlinked import (`AccountImportContext`, from the account job's download result).
  Lichess passes none.
* The next export therefore starts at month M+1 and never re-fetches a covered month; each
  stopped export that published at least one month advances the cursor strictly past the
  previous one (the previous cursor's month is `<= M`).
* The floor is not persisted separately: it is folded into the existing cursor value
  (`ProviderDownload.through_ms`, `coverage_cursor_ms`, `AccountSyncCursor`). No registry or
  schema change.

Correctness of the hand-off: the published prefix contains every game of the archives
`first_month..=M`; the next export starts at M+1; months that are still open (the current
month) are never covered by a floor, because a stop before the current month leaves it as M+1
or later, and the last chunk, which reaches the current month, reports no floor and keeps
today's newest-start cursor.

# Plan: Chess.com account sync of a very large history completes in bounded chunks

## Goal

A Chess.com account sync whose full history exceeds one export's byte, archive-count or time
bound imports that history instead of failing: each export publishes a complete prefix of whole
months, the existing coverage cursor records it, and the same account job continues from that
cursor until the history is covered. A failure after some chunks keeps the published chunks and
the next sync resumes from the cursor instead of starting over.

## Approach

### O1 — A resumable export publishes a complete prefix of whole months

Answers the MANDATE's "publish per month (or up to the last complete month under the cap) and
let the cursor carry the rest".

* The account path asks the Chess.com core for a *resumable* export. It stages months in
  ascending order and adds a month only whole: a month whose staged PGN would push the export
  past `MAX_PGN_BYTES` is not staged in this export, and staging stops there.
* `MAX_ARCHIVES` becomes a per-export bound on the number of months staged, not a refusal of an
  index that lists more months: staging stops after that many months. (Derived from "whole-run
  limits fail staging"; a player active every month since Chess.com's 2007 archives reaches 240
  months during 2027.) The index stays bounded by `MAX_INDEX_BYTES`.
* `EXPORT_TIMEOUT` bounds one export. When it elapses after at least one whole month is staged,
  the in-flight fetch is dropped and the staged months are published; when no month is staged
  yet, the export fails with today's `"Chess.com export deadline exceeded"` and publishes
  nothing.
* A resumable export that stopped for any of these three reasons publishes the staged prefix
  through the unchanged `install_staged_pgn_artifact` and receipt path and reports its coverage
  floor (O1a) on `RuntimeDownload`. An export that staged every requested month (Lichess, a
  complete Chess.com export, the last chunk) reports none.
* Only whole months are ever published. A stopped export that processed at least one whole month
  publishes even when the rule-set filter left zero games in those months (the zero-byte PGN
  that `staging_only_variant_games_publishes_readable_empty_pgn` already pins for a complete
  export); an export that processed no whole month publishes nothing. Per-game skipping and the
  existing empty-archive and no-archive errors are unchanged.
* The standalone `download_chess_com_games` command keeps whole-export semantics: over a limit
  it fails exactly as today and publishes nothing. The scope (complete vs resumable) is an
  explicit argument of the runtime, chosen by its two callers, not inferred from
  `fail_progress_on_receipt_error`.

### O1a — Coverage is recorded by archive month, not only by game start

A game-start cursor cannot say "months up to M are covered": a slow link or long daily games can
leave the newest start inside the cursor month, so the next export would re-fetch the same
prefix forever (r1 P1).

* A stopped export whose last whole month is M reports a coverage floor equal to 00:00 UTC on the
  first day of month M+1. All archives up to M were fetched whole and every game they hold
  started before that instant, so the floor is a true lower bound of the coverage the export
  establishes.
* The shared `account_sync_cursor` takes the floor as an input and returns at least the floor;
  with no floor its result is unchanged for every provider. Both cursor writers pass it: the
  linked receipt (`record_download` via `record_download_receipt`, from the Chess.com core) and
  the unlinked import (`AccountImportContext`, from the account job's download result).
  Lichess passes none.
* The next export therefore starts at month M+1 and never re-fetches a covered month; each
  stopped export that published at least one month advances the cursor strictly past the
  previous one (the previous cursor's month is `<= M`).
* The floor is not persisted separately: it is folded into the existing cursor value
  (`ProviderDownload.through_ms`, `coverage_cursor_ms`, `AccountSyncCursor`). No registry or
  schema change.

Correctness of the hand-off: the published prefix contains every game of the archives
`first_month..=M`; the next export starts at M+1; months that are still open (the current
month) are never covered by a floor, because a stop before the current month leaves it as M+1
or later, and the last chunk, which reaches the current month, reports no floor and keeps
today's newest-start cursor.

### O2 — The account job continues from the cursor until the history is covered

* For Chess.com, after a chunk has fully completed its existing lifecycle — linked:
  `append_collection_batch` returned with `index_ready && durability_confirmed` (so
  `finish_pending` advanced the cursor and cleared the pending batch); unlinked: conversion
  committed and `delete_empty_games_blocking` finished — the same account job starts the next
  chunk with a fresh download, still under the same lease and cancellation token, unless that
  chunk was produced by a download in this attempt that reported no floor.
* Consequently a chunk that this attempt recovered rather than downloaded (a `pending_batch`
  re-submission, or a `deletion_pending` retry of the same completion) is always followed by a
  fresh download from the cursor: the attempt cannot know whether the recovered chunk was the
  last, and the follow-up download is an ordinary incremental sync that ends the job when it
  reports no floor (r1 P3).
* The next chunk's `since_ms` is read natively after the previous chunk completed: linked from the
  collection record's current coverage cursor, through the same filter the descriptor uses for
  `fetch_since_ms` (one shared function, both callers routed through it); unlinked from
  `account_sync_cursor_blocking`, as the first chunk already does. The renderer-supplied
  `since_ms` is used only for the first linked chunk, as today.
* Each chunk resets the per-chunk snapshot state it owns (`publication`, `coverage_holds`,
  `committed_database`, `deletion_pending`, `importing`, the floor) and clears
  `request.pending_batch`, then takes a new download ticket, updates `progress_id` and emits
  `CompletionChanged` exactly as the first download does.
* The loop stops with success when a chunk downloaded in this attempt reports no floor. It stops
  with the existing notice result (`collection_notice: true`) when a linked append is committed
  but not `index_ready` or not `durability_confirmed`. Any chunk error ends the job with that
  error, as today; earlier chunks stay published with their cursor.
* Progress guard, applied only to continuation after a chunk downloaded in this attempt that
  reported a floor: the next chunk starts only if the native cursor after that chunk is strictly
  greater than the cursor it started from (absent counts as lowest). O1a makes this hold for
  every stopped export; if it ever does not, the job fails with a typed error naming that the
  Chess.com history could not advance, so a chunk can never repeat forever. The follow-up
  download after a recovered chunk is not guarded: a recovered complete incremental chunk may
  legitimately leave the cursor unchanged, and that follow-up is a single ordinary download that
  then obeys the same rules. The number of chunks is bounded by the number of archive months
  plus one.
* Lichess account jobs are unchanged: one download, no continuation.
* The download seam in `import_account_games_with_download` / `start_account_completion`
  becomes callable once per chunk; production `download_account` selects the resumable scope
  for Chess.com.

### O3 — Pending-batch recovery contract stays per chunk

Answers the MANDATE's "how does that interact with the pending-batch recovery contract?": every
chunk is one ordinary batch with its own receipt, pending record, publish, `finish_pending` and
cursor advance. No chunk starts while a pending batch exists, because O2 starts the next chunk
only after `finish_pending` succeeded. A failure inside chunk k leaves chunks `< k` published
with the cursor advanced, and chunk k in exactly the recovery state a single export leaves
today; the existing Retry finishes chunk k and, by O2, continues with the remaining history in
the same job. No new recovery state, registry field or renderer contract is introduced.

### O4 — Verification

Tests that fail on BASE show:
* a resumable export over the byte cap, over the per-export month cap, and past the deadline
  after one month each publishes exactly the whole leading months and reports the floor of the
  following month; a deadline before any month publishes nothing; a stopped export whose whole
  months hold only filtered rule sets publishes the empty PGN with a floor;
* `account_sync_cursor` returns at least the floor and is unchanged without one; the linked
  receipt and the unlinked `AccountSyncCursor` both carry it;
* a slow-link sequence (each export completes one month before its deadline) advances one
  month per chunk to the end, with no month fetched twice;
* the complete scope (standalone command) still fails over the cap and publishes nothing;
* the account job runs further chunks with the native cursor as `since_ms` and ends when a chunk
  downloaded in the attempt reports no floor (linked and unlinked); it stops without another
  download when the append is not `index_ready`, and fails with the typed error when the cursor
  did not advance;
* recovery across chunks (linked and unlinked): chunk 1 commits, chunk 2 fails after its batch
  is staged (linked) or after conversion committed and before cleanup (unlinked); the earlier
  chunk's games and cursor are preserved; Retry recovers chunk 2's own batch (not chunk 1's
  publication) and then continues to the remaining months in the same job; the final cursor
  covers the whole history; a recovered chunk that leaves the cursor unchanged is followed by
  exactly one fresh download and the job succeeds when that download reports no floor.

## Decisions and trade-offs

Decided autonomously (full auto; technical, no user-visible promise changes — a sync that today
fails now imports the history):

* **Q:** Where does the continuation live — in the account job, or does the user press Download
  again per chunk? **Chosen:** the native account job loops (O2). **Rejected:** one chunk per
  click (the renderer has no "more available" signal, so the user would see a successful sync
  with missing history); a renderer-driven loop (renderer state is not authoritative for native
  jobs, async-resource-invariants). **Reversal:** drop the loop; the cursor still carries the
  rest one click at a time.
* **Q:** Truncate at a game or at a month boundary? **Chosen:** whole months (O1). **Rejected:**
  game-level truncation (the receipt would describe a partial month). **Reversal:** none needed;
  month granularity is the provider's archive unit.
* **Q:** How does a stopped export record that its months are covered? **Chosen:** a month-start
  floor folded into the existing cursor through `account_sync_cursor` (O1a). **Rejected:** the
  unchanged game-start cursor (stalls on a slow link or long daily games, r1 P1); a separate
  persisted "covered month" field (a second cursor beside the one both paths already store and
  read). **Reversal:** pass no floor; the cursor returns to newest start.
* **Q:** What follows a recovered chunk? **Chosen:** a fresh download from the cursor (O2).
  **Rejected:** ending the job after recovery (silently incomplete history, r1 P3); persisting a
  "more available" flag with the pending batch (new registry state for what one incremental
  download answers). **Reversal:** end recovered attempts after one chunk.
* **Q:** What does the standalone `download_chess_com_games` file download do over a limit?
  **Chosen:** unchanged failure, nothing published (O1). **Rejected:** publishing a silently
  partial file. **Reversal:** route it through the resumable scope once it has a caller that can
  continue.
* **Q:** What happens to `MAX_ARCHIVES`? **Chosen:** a per-export month bound (O1).
  **Rejected:** keeping it as an index refusal (a long-lived account then never syncs), dropping
  it (the per-export work bound becomes byte- and time-only). **Reversal:** restore the index
  check.

## Risks / open questions

* The progress bar restarts for every chunk, as it already does between download and
  conversion; no new UI.
* A single archive month whose body takes longer than `EXPORT_TIMEOUT` still fails, as today;
  O1 needs one whole month per export.
* The floor treats a past month's archive as final once fetched. Chess.com archives a game under
  its end month, so a past month gains no new games; an archive that lags its month end by more
  than the time until the next month boundary is an unobserved provider behaviour and is not

# Plan: Chess.com account sync of a very large history completes in bounded chunks

## Goal

A Chess.com account sync whose full history exceeds one export's byte, archive-count or time
bound imports that history instead of failing: each export publishes a complete prefix of whole
months, the existing coverage cursor records it, and the same account job continues from that
cursor until the history is covered. A failure after some chunks keeps the published chunks and
the next sync resumes from the cursor instead of starting over.

## MANDATE

Finding f-20261101-29 (verbatim):

* **Where:** `src-tauri/src/chesscom.rs:44,411-416` (100 MiB `MAX_PGN_BYTES` over the whole export), `chesscom.rs:40,43` (15-minute export deadline, 16 MiB per month), `db/linked_collections/batch.rs:509-531` (cursor saved only after a successful publish).
* **Defect:** whole-run limits fail staging before anything is published, and without a cursor each retry refetches the full history.
* **Failure scenario:** an account with roughly 40,000+ games (about 1.5-2.5 KB each with clocks) gets "Chess.com PGN export is too large" on every Download and never imports any history.
* **Open question:** publish per month (or up to the last complete month under the cap) and let the cursor carry the rest; how does that interact with the pending-batch recovery contract?

## Threat model and non-goals

Input adversary: none beyond today's. Chess.com responses stay untrusted and keep every existing

(all platforms, the only account path on Windows). The real load is an honest account with
tens of thousands of games, or 20 years of monthly archives, on a slow or rate-limited link.
Non-goals: Lichess export limits; changing what a sync imports, how games are deduplicated, or
how the cursor is computed for Lichess or for an export that covered every requested month; any
renderer or UI change. (Amended in r1 by focused judgment on P1: the game-start cursor cannot

## Approach

### O1 — A resumable export publishes a complete prefix of whole months

Answers the MANDATE's "publish per month (or up to the last complete month under the cap) and
let the cursor carry the rest".

* The account path asks the Chess.com core for a *resumable* export. It stages months in
  ascending order and adds a month only whole: a month whose staged PGN would push the export
  past `MAX_PGN_BYTES` is not staged in this export, and staging stops there.
* `MAX_ARCHIVES` becomes a per-export bound on the number of months staged, not a refusal of an
  index that lists more months: staging stops after that many months. (Derived from "whole-run
  limits fail staging"; a player active every month since Chess.com's 2007 archives reaches 240
  months during 2027.) The index stays bounded by `MAX_INDEX_BYTES`.
* `EXPORT_TIMEOUT` bounds one export. When it elapses after at least one whole month is staged,
  the in-flight fetch is dropped and the staged months are published; when no month is staged
  yet, the export fails with today's `"Chess.com export deadline exceeded"` and publishes
  nothing.
* A resumable export that stopped for any of these three reasons publishes the staged prefix
  through the unchanged `install_staged_pgn_artifact` and receipt path and reports its coverage
  floor (O1a) on `RuntimeDownload`. An export that staged every requested month (Lichess, a
  complete Chess.com export, the last chunk) reports none.
* Only whole months are ever published. A stopped export that processed at least one whole month
  publishes even when the rule-set filter left zero games in those months (the zero-byte PGN
  that `staging_only_variant_games_publishes_readable_empty_pgn` already pins for a complete
  export); an export that processed no whole month publishes nothing. Per-game skipping and the
  existing empty-archive and no-archive errors are unchanged.
* The standalone `download_chess_com_games` command keeps whole-export semantics: over a limit
  it fails exactly as today and publishes nothing. The scope (complete vs resumable) is an
  explicit argument of the runtime, chosen by its two callers, not inferred from
  `fail_progress_on_receipt_error`.

### O1a — Coverage is recorded by archive month, not only by game start

A game-start cursor cannot say "months up to M are covered": a slow link or long daily games can
leave the newest start inside the cursor month, so the next export would re-fetch the same
prefix forever (r1 P1).

* A stopped export whose last whole month is M reports a coverage floor equal to 00:00 UTC on the
  first day of month M+1. All archives up to M were fetched whole and every game they hold
  started before that instant, so the floor is a true lower bound of the coverage the export
  establishes.
* The shared `account_sync_cursor` takes the floor as an input and returns at least the floor;
  with no floor its result is unchanged for every provider. Both cursor writers pass it: the
  linked receipt (`record_download` via `record_download_receipt`, from the Chess.com core) and
  the unlinked import (`AccountImportContext`, from the account job's download result).
  Lichess passes none.
* The next export therefore starts at month M+1 and never re-fetches a covered month; each
  stopped export that published at least one month advances the cursor strictly past the
  previous one (the previous cursor's month is `<= M`).
* The floor is not persisted separately: it is folded into the existing cursor value
  (`ProviderDownload.through_ms`, `coverage_cursor_ms`, `AccountSyncCursor`). No registry or
  schema change.

Correctness of the hand-off: the published prefix contains every game of the archives
`first_month..=M`; the next export starts at M+1; months that are still open (the current
month) are never covered by a floor, because a stop before the current month leaves it as M+1
or later, and the last chunk, which reaches the current month, reports no floor and keeps
today's newest-start cursor.

### O2 — The account job continues from the cursor until the history is covered

* For Chess.com, after a chunk has fully completed its existing lifecycle — linked:
  `append_collection_batch` returned with `index_ready && durability_confirmed` (so
  `finish_pending` advanced the cursor and cleared the pending batch); unlinked: conversion
  committed and `delete_empty_games_blocking` finished — the same account job starts the next
  chunk with a fresh download, still under the same lease and cancellation token, unless that
  chunk was produced by a download in this attempt that reported no floor.
* Consequently a chunk that this attempt recovered rather than downloaded (a `pending_batch`
  re-submission, or a `deletion_pending` retry of the same completion) is always followed by a
  fresh download from the cursor: the attempt cannot know whether the recovered chunk was the
  last, and the follow-up download is an ordinary incremental sync that ends the job when it
  reports no floor (r1 P3).
* The next chunk's `since_ms` is read natively after the previous chunk completed: linked from the
  collection record's current coverage cursor, through the same filter the descriptor uses for
  `fetch_since_ms` (one shared function, both callers routed through it); unlinked from
  `account_sync_cursor_blocking`, as the first chunk already does. The renderer-supplied
  `since_ms` is used only for the first linked chunk, as today.
* Each chunk resets the per-chunk snapshot state it owns (`publication`, `coverage_holds`,
  `committed_database`, `deletion_pending`, `importing`, the floor) and clears
  `request.pending_batch`, then takes a new download ticket, updates `progress_id` and emits
  `CompletionChanged` exactly as the first download does.
* The loop stops with success when a chunk downloaded in this attempt reports no floor. It stops
  with the existing notice result (`collection_notice: true`) when a linked append is committed
  but not `index_ready` or not `durability_confirmed`. Any chunk error ends the job with that
  error, as today; earlier chunks stay published with their cursor.
* Progress guard, applied only to continuation after a chunk downloaded in this attempt that
  reported a floor: the next chunk starts only if the native cursor after that chunk is strictly
  greater than the cursor it started from (absent counts as lowest). O1a makes this hold for
  every stopped export; if it ever does not, the job fails with a typed error naming that the
  Chess.com history could not advance, so a chunk can never repeat forever. The follow-up
  download after a recovered chunk is not guarded: a recovered complete incremental chunk may
  legitimately leave the cursor unchanged, and that follow-up is a single ordinary download that
  then obeys the same rules. The number of chunks is bounded by the number of archive months
  plus one.
* Lichess account jobs are unchanged: one download, no continuation.
* The download seam in `import_account_games_with_download` / `start_account_completion`
  becomes callable once per chunk; production `download_account` selects the resumable scope
  for Chess.com.

### O3 — Pending-batch recovery contract stays per chunk

Answers the MANDATE's "how does that interact with the pending-batch recovery contract?": every
chunk is one ordinary batch with its own receipt, pending record, publish, `finish_pending` and
cursor advance. No chunk starts while a pending batch exists, because O2 starts the next chunk
only after `finish_pending` succeeded. A failure inside chunk k leaves chunks `< k` published
with the cursor advanced, and chunk k in exactly the recovery state a single export leaves
today; the existing Retry finishes chunk k and, by O2, continues with the remaining history in
the same job. No new recovery state, registry field or renderer contract is introduced.

### O4 — Verification

Tests that fail on BASE show:
* a resumable export over the byte cap, over the per-export month cap, and past the deadline
  after one month each publishes exactly the whole leading months and reports the floor of the
  following month; a deadline before any month publishes nothing; a stopped export whose whole
  months hold only filtered rule sets publishes the empty PGN with a floor;
* `account_sync_cursor` returns at least the floor and is unchanged without one; the linked
  receipt and the unlinked `AccountSyncCursor` both carry it;
* a slow-link sequence (each export completes one month before its deadline) advances one
  month per chunk to the end, with no month fetched twice;
* the complete scope (standalone command) still fails over the cap and publishes nothing;
* the account job runs further chunks with the native cursor as `since_ms` and ends when a chunk
  downloaded in the attempt reports no floor (linked and unlinked); it stops without another
  download when the append is not `index_ready`, and fails with the typed error when the cursor
  did not advance;
* recovery across chunks (linked and unlinked): chunk 1 commits, chunk 2 fails after its batch
  is staged (linked) or after conversion committed and before cleanup (unlinked); the earlier
  chunk's games and cursor are preserved; Retry recovers chunk 2's own batch (not chunk 1's
  publication) and then continues to the remaining months in the same job; the final cursor
  covers the whole history; a recovered chunk that leaves the cursor unchanged is followed by
  exactly one fresh download and the job succeeds when that download reports no floor.

## Decisions and trade-offs

Decided autonomously (full auto; technical, no user-visible promise changes — a sync that today
fails now imports the history):

* **Q:** Where does the continuation live — in the account job, or does the user press Download
  again per chunk? **Chosen:** the native account job loops (O2). **Rejected:** one chunk per
  click (the renderer has no "more available" signal, so the user would see a successful sync
  with missing history); a renderer-driven loop (renderer state is not authoritative for native
  jobs, async-resource-invariants). **Reversal:** drop the loop; the cursor still carries the
  rest one click at a time.
* **Q:** Truncate at a game or at a month boundary? **Chosen:** whole months (O1). **Rejected:**
  game-level truncation (the receipt would describe a partial month). **Reversal:** none needed;
  month granularity is the provider's archive unit.
* **Q:** How does a stopped export record that its months are covered? **Chosen:** a month-start
  floor folded into the existing cursor through `account_sync_cursor` (O1a). **Rejected:** the
  unchanged game-start cursor (stalls on a slow link or long daily games, r1 P1); a separate
  persisted "covered month" field (a second cursor beside the one both paths already store and
  read). **Reversal:** pass no floor; the cursor returns to newest start.
* **Q:** What follows a recovered chunk? **Chosen:** a fresh download from the cursor (O2).
  **Rejected:** ending the job after recovery (silently incomplete history, r1 P3); persisting a
  "more available" flag with the pending batch (new registry state for what one incremental
  download answers). **Reversal:** end recovered attempts after one chunk.
* **Q:** What does the standalone `download_chess_com_games` file download do over a limit?
  **Chosen:** unchanged failure, nothing published (O1). **Rejected:** publishing a silently
  partial file. **Reversal:** route it through the resumable scope once it has a caller that can
  continue.
* **Q:** What happens to `MAX_ARCHIVES`? **Chosen:** a per-export month bound (O1).
  **Rejected:** keeping it as an index refusal (a long-lived account then never syncs), dropping
  it (the per-export work bound becomes byte- and time-only). **Reversal:** restore the index
  check.

## Risks / open questions

* The progress bar restarts for every chunk, as it already does between download and
  conversion; no new UI.
* A single archive month whose body takes longer than `EXPORT_TIMEOUT` still fails, as today;
  O1 needs one whole month per export.
* The floor treats a past month's archive as final once fetched. Chess.com archives a game under
  its end month, so a past month gains no new games; an archive that lags its month end by more
  than the time until the next month boundary is an unobserved provider behaviour and is not
  handled.
