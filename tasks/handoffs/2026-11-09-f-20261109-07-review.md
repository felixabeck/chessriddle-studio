# Plan: a pooled acquire must not lose its replenishment request to a sibling (f-20261109-07)

## Goal

A caller of `DatabaseEntry::get_connection_cancellable` that finds no idle connection always has a
replenishment request outstanding until it obtains a connection, fails, is cancelled or reaches its
deadline. A sibling that takes the connection this caller requested can no longer leave the caller
waiting for the full pool timeout. `upgrade_tests::validated_connections_skip_schema_probes_and_reprobe_changed_files`
becomes deterministically green on every platform without changing its deadline or assertion.

## MANDATE

From f-20261109-07 (verbatim):

* **Defect:** `f-20261108-08` and `f-20261108-10` closed the earlier reds as a slow runner and replaced the 2 s per-reader bound with one 30 s deadline. The test is red again on the hosted macOS runner. Three readers connected within 1.7–6.1 ms, and reader 1 had not connected after 30 s. When the test then released the three held connections, every reader thread joined and returned, because `reader.join().unwrap()` did not panic before the assert. So reader 1's `connection()` was blocked until siblings released their connections. That is serialization or lock contention between concurrent pooled acquires, not a slow runner.
* **Failure scenario:** Four threads open the same validated database concurrently and each holds its connection. On macOS one acquire blocks until the others release. In the app, a long-held reader (for example a search) can stall a concurrent database read for the r2d2 timeout. CI on `master` is red intermittently (`3859bcd9` and `8c6308ed` on 2026-11-09).
* **Open question:** Which lock or pool step serializes a concurrent validated acquire behind held sibling connections, and where does WAL/PRAGMA setup belong so acquires stay independent?
* **Fix:** Find the blocking mechanism with the received/missing diagnostics the test already emits, plus per-phase timing inside `connection()` (pool get versus `on_acquire` PRAGMAs). Make a concurrent validated acquire independent of held sibling connections, for example by setting WAL once at entry creation instead of on every acquire if that is the lock. Keep the test's assertion that every reader connects while its siblings hold connections. Do not raise the deadline again.
* **Proof:** CI log of job `rust-macos-test` in run 42601458748: `received [(0, true, 1.664418ms), (3, true, 5.231171ms), (2, true, 6.118579ms)], missing 1 readers`, then 3150 passed and 1 failed. The same test is green on Linux locally.

Obligations derived from it:

* M1 — answer the open question with the evidenced mechanism.
* M2 — make a concurrent validated acquire independent of held sibling connections.
* M3 — keep the test's every-reader-connects assertion, and do not raise its 30 s deadline.

## Approach

### A. Mechanism (answers M1)

The blocking step is the lost replenishment request in `get_connection_cancellable`. It is not a
SQLite lock, PRAGMA, VFS or r2d2 worker issue. The `on_acquire` PRAGMAs stay where they are. The
answer and the probe output go into the finding's closing note.

### B. Liveness contract for a waiting acquire (M2)

`get_connection_cancellable` keeps one invariant. While a caller waits without a connection, it
re-arms replenishment with one more `pool.get_timeout(Duration::ZERO)` whenever the pool has
established a connection since the caller's last request and that caller did not obtain it.

* **Establishment counter.** `PoolWake` gets a monotonic count of successful establishments.
  `PoolEvents::handle_acquire` increments it in the same critical section that bumps `generation`,
  so a waiter that samples `generation` cannot miss the increment. Checkins and errors do not
  increment it.
* **Caller bookkeeping.** The caller samples the count immediately **before** each request. On a
  later iteration with no connection, if the count differs from its sample, it re-samples and
  re-requests. Without a new establishment it keeps today's `try_get`-only polling. So a slow or
  failing establishment never makes a waiter schedule a worker on every 25 ms poll, and the
  existing "sole replenishment request" test keeps its meaning.
* **Bounded over-provisioning.** A re-arm can over-provision. This happens when the caller's own
  request is still pending while another caller's connection was taken, or in r2d2's window
  between `handle_acquire` and the idle push. Each re-arm needs a distinct observed establishment,
  so there is at most one extra request per waiter per establishment. r2d2 caps the total at
  `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_DATABASE` (16) through `add_connection`. Surplus idle connections are
  reaped by the existing r2d2 idle timeout, which `d-20261011-15` keeps on purpose. It rejected
  `idle_timeout(None)` because that would keep up to 16 idle handles open. Losing
  liveness is the defect, and a bounded surplus is the accepted cost.
* **Failure and diagnostic semantics are unchanged.** Cancellation, the absolute `deadline`,
  predecessor admission on every iteration, `pool_deadline`, and native-open failure arbitration
  against `pool_wake.failure` behave as today. A re-arm's `Err` may update the caller's
  `pool_failure` only with a non-empty newer r2d2 error, so it never replaces a recorded failure
  with an empty one. The comment at :3103-3104 is rewritten to state the new contract.
* **One owner.** The re-arm lives only in `get_connection_cancellable`, so both production callers
  (:1120 and :2448) get it. No second copy is added.

### C. Regression proof (M2, M3)

* **New deterministic unit test** in `repository.rs` tests. Caller A enters
  `get_connection_cancellable` on an entry whose pool has no idle connection, after an earlier
  connection is held by the test. A `#[cfg(test)]` hook runs on A's thread right after its first
  request. Inside the hook, the test waits until the pool's state shows an idle connection, then
  takes it as the "sibling" through `entry.get_connection()`, which pops without scheduling. Then
  the hook returns. A must return `Ok` while every sibling still holds its connection. The test uses
  a repository built with `with_pool_connection_timeout(SHORT_POOL_CONNECTION_TIMEOUT)` or an
  equivalent short bound, so the BASE behaviour fails fast as a timeout. The ordering comes from
  the hook and the pool state, never from sleeps. Red on BASE, green after B.
* The new hook is one `TestHook` variant, wired through the existing `run_test_hook` /
  `run_noarg_test_hook` registry like `PoolBeforeFailureArbitration`.
* **Existing test unchanged in substance.**
  `validated_connections_skip_schema_probes_and_reprobe_changed_files` keeps
  `CONNECT_TIMEOUT = 30 s` and its every-reader-connects assertion (M3). Its own logic needs no
  change.
* **Existing pool tests stay green unchanged.** In particular, the single-request tests at
  :10824-10900, `pool_wake_preserves_a_notification_published_before_wait`, and the failure
  arbitration and recovery tests.

## Decisions and trade-offs

Decided autonomously (full auto). The adopter records these through `findings.py record-decision`:

* **Question:** how does a waiting pooled acquire keep a replenishment request outstanding when a
  sibling can take the connection it requested?
  **Chosen:** re-arm one `get_timeout(ZERO)` per observed establishment the caller did not win,
  counted by a monotonic establishment counter in `PoolWake`.
  **Rejected:**
  * Moving `PRAGMA journal_mode = WAL` out of `on_acquire`. The probe and the r2d2 source show the
    stall without any SQLite involvement, and that change would not touch the cause.
  * Calling `get_timeout(slice)` on every poll. r2d2 calls `add_connection` on every empty call, so
    a slow establishment would schedule up to 16 workers per waiter.
  * `min_idle(Some(n))`. It only narrows the window, keeps idle handles open, and conflicts with
    the per-pool lazy-establishment design.
  * Re-requesting on every wake, including checkins and errors. A failing establishment would then
    fan out one worker per waiter per error.
  * Raising the test deadline or relaxing the assertion. M3 forbids both.

  **Reason:** this is the smallest change that restores the per-waiter liveness invariant, keeps a
  single owner for acquisition, and leaves failure arbitration untouched.
  **Reversal path:** if r2d2 gains a requester-bound acquire, or the pool moves to
  `min_idle` = demand, remove the counter and the re-arm branch, and keep the regression test.

## Risks / open questions

* The `handle_acquire`-before-push window can cause one spurious re-arm. This is bounded and
  covered by the 16-connection cap and the idle reaper.
* r2d2 0.8.10 internals are the premise. A future r2d2 bump must re-read `get_timeout` and
  `try_get_inner`, and the new test will fail if the semantics change in a way that matters.

## Approach

### A. Mechanism (answers M1)

The blocking step is the lost replenishment request in `get_connection_cancellable`. It is not a
SQLite lock, PRAGMA, VFS or r2d2 worker issue. The `on_acquire` PRAGMAs stay where they are. The
answer and the probe output go into the finding's closing note.

### B. Liveness contract for a waiting acquire (M2)

`get_connection_cancellable` keeps one invariant. While a caller waits without a connection, it
re-arms replenishment with one more `pool.get_timeout(Duration::ZERO)` whenever the pool has
established a connection since the caller's last request and that caller did not obtain it.

* **Establishment counter.** `PoolWake` gets a monotonic count of successful establishments.
  `PoolEvents::handle_acquire` increments it in the same critical section that bumps `generation`,
  so a waiter that samples `generation` cannot miss the increment. Checkins and errors do not
  increment it.
* **Caller bookkeeping.** The caller samples the count immediately **before** each request. On a
  later iteration with no connection, if the count differs from its sample, it re-samples and
  re-requests. Without a new establishment it keeps today's `try_get`-only polling. So a slow or
  failing establishment never makes a waiter schedule a worker on every 25 ms poll, and the
  existing "sole replenishment request" test keeps its meaning.
* **Bounded over-provisioning.** A re-arm can over-provision. This happens when the caller's own
  request is still pending while another caller's connection was taken, or in r2d2's window
  between `handle_acquire` and the idle push. Each re-arm needs a distinct observed establishment,
  so there is at most one extra request per waiter per establishment. r2d2 caps the total at
  `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_DATABASE` (16) through `add_connection`. Surplus idle connections are
  reaped by the existing r2d2 idle timeout, which `d-20261011-15` keeps on purpose. It rejected
  `idle_timeout(None)` because that would keep up to 16 idle handles open. Losing
  liveness is the defect, and a bounded surplus is the accepted cost.
* **Failure and diagnostic semantics are unchanged.** Cancellation, the absolute `deadline`,
  predecessor admission on every iteration, `pool_deadline`, and native-open failure arbitration
  against `pool_wake.failure` behave as today. A re-arm's `Err` may update the caller's
  `pool_failure` only with a non-empty newer r2d2 error, so it never replaces a recorded failure
  with an empty one. The comment at :3103-3104 is rewritten to state the new contract.
* **One owner.** The re-arm lives only in `get_connection_cancellable`, so both production callers
  (:1120 and :2448) get it. No second copy is added.

## Decisions and trade-offs

Decided autonomously (full auto). The adopter records these through `findings.py record-decision`:

* **Question:** how does a waiting pooled acquire keep a replenishment request outstanding when a
  sibling can take the connection it requested?
  **Chosen:** re-arm one `get_timeout(ZERO)` per observed establishment the caller did not win,
  counted by a monotonic establishment counter in `PoolWake`.
  **Rejected:**
  * Moving `PRAGMA journal_mode = WAL` out of `on_acquire`. The probe and the r2d2 source show the
    stall without any SQLite involvement, and that change would not touch the cause.
  * Calling `get_timeout(slice)` on every poll. r2d2 calls `add_connection` on every empty call, so
    a slow establishment would schedule up to 16 workers per waiter.
  * `min_idle(Some(n))`. It only narrows the window, keeps idle handles open, and conflicts with
    the per-pool lazy-establishment design.
  * Re-requesting on every wake, including checkins and errors. A failing establishment would then
    fan out one worker per waiter per error.
  * Raising the test deadline or relaxing the assertion. M3 forbids both.

  **Reason:** this is the smallest change that restores the per-waiter liveness invariant, keeps a
  single owner for acquisition, and leaves failure arbitration untouched.
  **Reversal path:** if r2d2 gains a requester-bound acquire, or the pool moves to
  `min_idle` = demand, remove the counter and the re-arm branch, and keep the regression test.

* **Question:** how does a waiting pooled acquire keep a replenishment request outstanding when a

## Approach

### A. Mechanism (answers M1)

The blocking step is the lost replenishment request in `get_connection_cancellable`. It is not a
SQLite lock, PRAGMA, VFS or r2d2 worker issue. The `on_acquire` PRAGMAs stay where they are. The
answer and the probe output go into the finding's closing note.

### B. Liveness contract for a waiting acquire (M2)

`get_connection_cancellable` keeps one invariant. While a caller waits without a connection, it
re-arms replenishment with one more `pool.get_timeout(Duration::ZERO)` whenever the pool has
established a connection since the caller's last request and that caller did not obtain it.

* **Establishment counter.** `PoolWake` gets a monotonic count of successful establishments.
  `PoolEvents::handle_acquire` increments it in the same critical section that bumps `generation`,
  so a waiter that samples `generation` cannot miss the increment. Checkins and errors do not
  increment it.
* **Caller bookkeeping.** The caller samples the count immediately **before** each request. On a
  later iteration with no connection, if the count differs from its sample, it re-samples and
  re-requests. Without a new establishment it keeps today's `try_get`-only polling. So a slow or
  failing establishment never makes a waiter schedule a worker on every 25 ms poll, and the
  existing "sole replenishment request" test keeps its meaning.
* **Bounded over-provisioning.** A re-arm can over-provision. This happens when the caller's own
  request is still pending while another caller's connection was taken, or in r2d2's window
  between `handle_acquire` and the idle push. Each re-arm needs a distinct observed establishment,
  so there is at most one extra request per waiter per establishment. r2d2 caps the total at
  `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_DATABASE` (16) through `add_connection`. Surplus idle connections are
  reaped by the existing r2d2 idle timeout, which `d-20261011-15` keeps on purpose. It rejected
  `idle_timeout(None)` because that would keep up to 16 idle handles open. Losing
  liveness is the defect, and a bounded surplus is the accepted cost.
* **Failure and diagnostic semantics are unchanged.** Cancellation, the absolute `deadline`,
  predecessor admission on every iteration, `pool_deadline`, and native-open failure arbitration
  against `pool_wake.failure` behave as today. A re-arm's `Err` may update the caller's
  `pool_failure` only with a non-empty newer r2d2 error, so it never replaces a recorded failure
  with an empty one. The comment at :3103-3104 is rewritten to state the new contract.
* **One owner.** The re-arm lives only in `get_connection_cancellable`, so both production callers
  (:1120 and :2448) get it. No second copy is added.

### C. Regression proof (M2, M3)

* **New deterministic unit test** in `repository.rs` tests. Caller A enters
  `get_connection_cancellable` on an entry whose pool has no idle connection, after an earlier
  connection is held by the test. A `#[cfg(test)]` hook runs on A's thread right after its first
  request. Inside the hook, the test waits until the pool's state shows an idle connection, then
  takes it as the "sibling" through `entry.get_connection()`, which pops without scheduling. Then
  the hook returns. A must return `Ok` while every sibling still holds its connection. The test uses
  a repository built with `with_pool_connection_timeout(SHORT_POOL_CONNECTION_TIMEOUT)` or an
  equivalent short bound, so the BASE behaviour fails fast as a timeout. The ordering comes from
  the hook and the pool state, never from sleeps. Red on BASE, green after B.
* **New deterministic no-surplus test** (P1). It proves B's negative half: wakes without a new
  establishment schedule no additional replenishment. Setup:
  * A caller enters `get_connection_cancellable` on an entry with no idle connection.
  * Its first establishment is held at the existing one-shot `TestHook::WorkerBeforeEstablish`
    by-path hook. Holding needs only the first call, so one-shot is enough here.
  * **Observed quantity: the caller's own replenishment requests** (r4 correction, after the
    second failed closure). The test does not count `connect` entries on r2d2 worker threads. It
    counts each replenishment request `get_connection_cancellable` makes, meaning each call to
    `pool.get_timeout(Duration::ZERO)`. A `#[cfg(test)]` per-entry counter that is never consumed
    is incremented on the caller's thread immediately before each such call, following the
    `schema_probe_count` pattern. This is the decision under test: B's contract is about when the
    waiter requests replenishment, and r2d2's `add_connection` turns each request into at most one
    scheduled job. Worker-side counting cannot be ordered against a dequeued but not yet started
    job (scheduled-thread-pool 0.2.7 `lib.rs:381` and `:417`, review-plan and review-tests r3).
    The one-shot hooks cannot count at all (`repository.rs:2931-2965`, r2).
  * **Repeatable loop observer, ordered after the counter.** A `#[cfg(test)]` repeatable signal
    fires on the caller's thread at the end of every loop iteration that finds no connection,
    after any request that iteration made. Examples are a counter with a condvar, or a hook kept
    registered across calls. Both the request increment and the signal happen on the caller's
    thread in program order, and the test reads the counter after observing the signal through
    that observer's mutex. So every request made up to the observed iteration is visible to the
    test, with no scheduler involvement.
  * While the establishment is held, the test publishes several non-native pool errors through
    `PoolEvents::handle_error` with a `Customize` or `Validate` error. These bump `generation` but
    are not native-open, so they do not end the wait (`repository.rs:566-584` and `:3128`). After
    each error, the test waits on the loop observer until the caller has completed an iteration
    that began after that error. It also waits for at least two timeout-only iterations
    (`RETIRE_CANCELLATION_POLL` expiries). No sleeps.

  It asserts that the request counter equals 1. Then it releases the hook, and the caller must
  return `Ok`. The phase demonstrates that the test rejects the faulty behaviour: it temporarily
  re-requests on every loop iteration, records the red output, and reverts. The test must go red
  for an implementation that re-arms on every wake, on every error, or on every poll. It is green
  after B. On BASE it has no counter to read, so its first run is after the counter lands.
* The steal regression test above also asserts, through the same request counter, that the
  stolen-from caller made at least two requests: its first, plus a re-arm after the
  establishment it lost. The bound is "at least" because B allows one spurious re-arm in r2d2's
  `handle_acquire`-before-push window, so an exact count would be scheduling-dependent.
* The new hook is one `TestHook` variant, wired through the existing `run_test_hook` /
  `run_noarg_test_hook` registry like `PoolBeforeFailureArbitration`.
* **Existing test unchanged in substance.**
  `validated_connections_skip_schema_probes_and_reprobe_changed_files` keeps
  `CONNECT_TIMEOUT = 30 s` and its every-reader-connects assertion (M3). Its own logic needs no

* **Existing pool tests stay green unchanged.** In particular, the single-request tests at

## Approach

### A. Mechanism (answers M1)

The blocking step is the lost replenishment request in `get_connection_cancellable`. It is not a
SQLite lock, PRAGMA, VFS or r2d2 worker issue. The `on_acquire` PRAGMAs stay where they are. The
answer and the probe output go into the finding's closing note.

### B. Liveness contract for a waiting acquire (M2)

`get_connection_cancellable` keeps one invariant. While a caller waits without a connection, it
re-arms replenishment with one more `pool.get_timeout(Duration::ZERO)` whenever the pool has
established a connection since the caller's last request and that caller did not obtain it.

* **Establishment counter.** `PoolWake` gets a monotonic count of successful establishments.
  `PoolEvents::handle_acquire` increments it in the same critical section that bumps `generation`,
  so a waiter that samples `generation` cannot miss the increment. Checkins and errors do not
  increment it.
* **Caller bookkeeping.** The caller samples the count immediately **before** each request. On a
  later iteration with no connection, if the count differs from its sample, it re-samples and
  re-requests. Without a new establishment it keeps today's `try_get`-only polling. So a slow or
  failing establishment never makes a waiter schedule a worker on every 25 ms poll, and the
  existing "sole replenishment request" test keeps its meaning.
* **Bounded over-provisioning.** A re-arm can over-provision. This happens when the caller's own
  request is still pending while another caller's connection was taken, or in r2d2's window
  between `handle_acquire` and the idle push. Each re-arm needs a distinct observed establishment,
  so there is at most one extra request per waiter per establishment. r2d2 caps the total at
  `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_DATABASE` (16) through `add_connection`. Surplus idle connections are
  reaped by the existing r2d2 idle timeout, which `d-20261011-15` keeps on purpose. It rejected
  `idle_timeout(None)` because that would keep up to 16 idle handles open. Losing
  liveness is the defect, and a bounded surplus is the accepted cost.
* **Failure and diagnostic semantics are unchanged.** Cancellation, the absolute `deadline`,
  predecessor admission on every iteration, `pool_deadline`, and native-open failure arbitration
  against `pool_wake.failure` behave as today. A re-arm's `Err` may update the caller's
  `pool_failure` only with a non-empty newer r2d2 error, so it never replaces a recorded failure
  with an empty one. The comment at :3103-3104 is rewritten to state the new contract.
* **One owner.** The re-arm lives only in `get_connection_cancellable`, so both production callers
  (:1120 and :2448) get it. No second copy is added.

## Decisions and trade-offs

Decided autonomously (full auto). The adopter records these through `findings.py record-decision`:

* **Question:** how does a waiting pooled acquire keep a replenishment request outstanding when a
  sibling can take the connection it requested?
  **Chosen:** re-arm one `get_timeout(ZERO)` per observed establishment the caller did not win,
  counted by a monotonic establishment counter in `PoolWake`.
  **Rejected:**
  * Moving `PRAGMA journal_mode = WAL` out of `on_acquire`. The probe and the r2d2 source show the
    stall without any SQLite involvement, and that change would not touch the cause.
  * Calling `get_timeout(slice)` on every poll. r2d2 calls `add_connection` on every empty call, so
    a slow establishment would schedule up to 16 workers per waiter.
  * `min_idle(Some(n))`. It only narrows the window, keeps idle handles open, and conflicts with
    the per-pool lazy-establishment design.
  * Re-requesting on every wake, including checkins and errors. A failing establishment would then
    fan out one worker per waiter per error.
  * Raising the test deadline or relaxing the assertion. M3 forbids both.

  **Reason:** this is the smallest change that restores the per-waiter liveness invariant, keeps a
  single owner for acquisition, and leaves failure arbitration untouched.
  **Reversal path:** if r2d2 gains a requester-bound acquire, or the pool moves to
  `min_idle` = demand, remove the counter and the re-arm branch, and keep the regression test.

## Approach

### A. Mechanism (answers M1)

The blocking step is the lost replenishment request in `get_connection_cancellable`. It is not a
SQLite lock, PRAGMA, VFS or r2d2 worker issue. The `on_acquire` PRAGMAs stay where they are. The
answer and the probe output go into the finding's closing note.

### B. Liveness contract for a waiting acquire (M2)

`get_connection_cancellable` keeps one invariant. While a caller waits without a connection, it
re-arms replenishment with one more `pool.get_timeout(Duration::ZERO)` whenever the pool has
established a connection since the caller's last request and that caller did not obtain it.

* **Establishment counter.** `PoolWake` gets a monotonic count of successful establishments.
  `PoolEvents::handle_acquire` increments it in the same critical section that bumps `generation`,
  so a waiter that samples `generation` cannot miss the increment. Checkins and errors do not
  increment it.
* **Caller bookkeeping.** The caller samples the count immediately **before** each request. On a
  later iteration with no connection, if the count differs from its sample, it re-samples and
  re-requests. Without a new establishment it keeps today's `try_get`-only polling. So a slow or
  failing establishment never makes a waiter schedule a worker on every 25 ms poll, and the
  existing "sole replenishment request" test keeps its meaning.
* **Bounded over-provisioning.** A re-arm can over-provision. This happens when the caller's own
  request is still pending while another caller's connection was taken, or in r2d2's window
  between `handle_acquire` and the idle push. Each re-arm needs a distinct observed establishment,
  so there is at most one extra request per waiter per establishment. r2d2 caps the total at
  `MAX_CONNECTIONS_PER_DATABASE` (16) through `add_connection`. Surplus idle connections are
  reaped by the existing r2d2 idle timeout, which `d-20261011-15` keeps on purpose. It rejected
  `idle_timeout(None)` because that would keep up to 16 idle handles open. Losing
  liveness is the defect, and a bounded surplus is the accepted cost.
* **Failure and diagnostic semantics are unchanged.** Cancellation, the absolute `deadline`,
  predecessor admission on every iteration, `pool_deadline`, and native-open failure arbitration
  against `pool_wake.failure` behave as today. A re-arm's `Err` may update the caller's
  `pool_failure` only with a non-empty newer r2d2 error, so it never replaces a recorded failure
  with an empty one. The comment at :3103-3104 is rewritten to state the new contract.
* **One owner.** The re-arm lives only in `get_connection_cancellable`, so both production callers
  (:1120 and :2448) get it. No second copy is added.

### C. Regression proof (M2, M3)

* **New deterministic unit test** in `repository.rs` tests. Caller A enters
  `get_connection_cancellable` on an entry whose pool has no idle connection, after an earlier
  connection is held by the test. A `#[cfg(test)]` hook runs on A's thread right after its first
  request. Inside the hook, the test waits until the pool's state shows an idle connection, then
  takes it as the "sibling" through `entry.get_connection()`, which pops without scheduling. Then
  the hook returns. A must return `Ok` while every sibling still holds its connection. The test uses
  a repository built with `with_pool_connection_timeout(SHORT_POOL_CONNECTION_TIMEOUT)` or an
  equivalent short bound, so the BASE behaviour fails fast as a timeout. The ordering comes from
  the hook and the pool state, never from sleeps. Red on BASE, green after B.
* **New deterministic no-surplus test** (P1). It proves B's negative half: wakes without a new
  establishment schedule no additional replenishment. Setup:
  * A caller enters `get_connection_cancellable` on an entry with no idle connection.
  * Its first establishment is held at the existing one-shot `TestHook::WorkerBeforeEstablish`
    by-path hook. Holding needs only the first call, so one-shot is enough here.
  * **Observed quantity: the caller's own replenishment requests** (r4 correction, after the
    second failed closure). The test does not count `connect` entries on r2d2 worker threads. It
    counts each replenishment request `get_connection_cancellable` makes, meaning each call to
    `pool.get_timeout(Duration::ZERO)`. A `#[cfg(test)]` per-entry counter that is never consumed
    is incremented on the caller's thread immediately before each such call, following the
    `schema_probe_count` pattern. This is the decision under test: B's contract is about when the
    waiter requests replenishment, and r2d2's `add_connection` turns each request into at most one
    scheduled job. Worker-side counting cannot be ordered against a dequeued but not yet started
    job (scheduled-thread-pool 0.2.7 `lib.rs:381` and `:417`, review-plan and review-tests r3).
    The one-shot hooks cannot count at all (`repository.rs:2931-2965`, r2).
  * **Repeatable loop observer, ordered after the counter.** A `#[cfg(test)]` repeatable signal
    fires on the caller's thread at the end of every loop iteration that finds no connection,
    after any request that iteration made. Examples are a counter with a condvar, or a hook kept
    registered across calls. Both the request increment and the signal happen on the caller's
    thread in program order, and the test reads the counter after observing the signal through
    that observer's mutex. So every request made up to the observed iteration is visible to the
    test, with no scheduler involvement.
  * While the establishment is held, the test publishes several non-native pool errors through
    `PoolEvents::handle_error` with a `Customize` or `Validate` error. These bump `generation` but
    are not native-open, so they do not end the wait (`repository.rs:566-584` and `:3128`). After
    each error, the test waits on the loop observer until the caller has completed an iteration
    that began after that error. It also waits for at least two timeout-only iterations
    (`RETIRE_CANCELLATION_POLL` expiries). No sleeps.

  It asserts that the request counter equals 1. Then it releases the hook, and the caller must
  return `Ok`. The phase demonstrates that the test rejects the faulty behaviour: it temporarily
  re-requests on every loop iteration, records the red output, and reverts. The test must go red
  for an implementation that re-arms on every wake, on every error, or on every poll. It is green
  after B. On BASE it has no counter to read, so its first run is after the counter lands.
* **Steal test continuation: re-sample and checkin** (r5). The steal regression test continues
  after the steal, using the same request counter and loop observer:
  * **Hold the replacement.** Before releasing A's hook, the test registers the one-shot
    `WorkerBeforeEstablish` hook so that it holds the next establishment. That is A's re-armed
    replacement. While it is held, no `handle_acquire` fires. So B's spurious re-arm window cannot
    occur, and every count below is exact.
  * After A resumes and completes its next iteration, the request counter is exactly 2: the
    first request plus one re-arm after the lost establishment.
  * **Re-sample.** Several non-native error wakes and at least two timeout-only iterations
    follow, each observed through the loop observer. The counter must stay at 2. This rejects an
    implementation that re-arms without updating its establishment sample.
  * **Checkin retaken by a sibling.** The loop observer can also pause A at the end of an
    iteration. A repeatable callback the test controls is enough. While A is paused, the test
    checks a held connection back in (a `CheckinEvent` that bumps `generation`), immediately takes
    it again through `entry.get_connection()`, and then unpauses A. A wakes, finds nothing, and
    completes an iteration. The counter must stay at 2. This rejects an implementation that counts
    checkins as establishments (r2d2 fires `handle_checkin` before the idle push,
    `r2d2-0.8.10/src/lib.rs:494`).
  * The test then releases the held replacement, and A must return `Ok` while its siblings still
    hold their connections.
  * The phase records red output for three faulty variants, then reverts each: re-arm on every
    iteration, no re-sample after a re-arm, and counting checkins.
* The new hook is one `TestHook` variant, wired through the existing `run_test_hook` /
  `run_noarg_test_hook` registry like `PoolBeforeFailureArbitration`.
* **Existing test unchanged in substance.**
  `validated_connections_skip_schema_probes_and_reprobe_changed_files` keeps
  `CONNECT_TIMEOUT = 30 s` and its every-reader-connects assertion (M3). Its own logic needs no
  change.
* **Existing pool tests stay green unchanged.** In particular, the single-request tests at
  :10824-10900, `pool_wake_preserves_a_notification_published_before_wait`, and the failure
  arbitration and recovery tests.

## Decisions and trade-offs

Decided autonomously (full auto). The adopter records these through `findings.py record-decision`:

* **Question:** how does a waiting pooled acquire keep a replenishment request outstanding when a
  sibling can take the connection it requested?
  **Chosen:** re-arm one `get_timeout(ZERO)` per observed establishment the caller did not win,
  counted by a monotonic establishment counter in `PoolWake`.
  **Rejected:**
  * Moving `PRAGMA journal_mode = WAL` out of `on_acquire`. The probe and the r2d2 source show the
    stall without any SQLite involvement, and that change would not touch the cause.
  * Calling `get_timeout(slice)` on every poll. r2d2 calls `add_connection` on every empty call, so
    a slow establishment would schedule up to 16 workers per waiter.
  * `min_idle(Some(n))`. It only narrows the window, keeps idle handles open, and conflicts with
    the per-pool lazy-establishment design.
  * Re-requesting on every wake, including checkins and errors. A failing establishment would then
    fan out one worker per waiter per error.
  * Raising the test deadline or relaxing the assertion. M3 forbids both.

  **Reason:** this is the smallest change that restores the per-waiter liveness invariant, keeps a
  single owner for acquisition, and leaves failure arbitration untouched.
  **Reversal path:** if r2d2 gains a requester-bound acquire, or the pool moves to
  `min_idle` = demand, remove the counter and the re-arm branch, and keep the regression test.

## Risks / open questions

* The `handle_acquire`-before-push window can cause one spurious re-arm. This is bounded and
  covered by the 16-connection cap and the idle reaper.
* r2d2 0.8.10 internals are the premise. A future r2d2 bump must re-read `get_timeout` and
  `try_get_inner`, and the new test will fail if the semantics change in a way that matters.
